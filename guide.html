Hunting Teeth on Oak Island

Braden’s best tips for walking away with a handful.

  1. Go at low tide

    Start about two hours before low tide. The falling water uncovers fresh shell beds right at the water’s edge.

  2. Hunt after a storm

    Big waves and strong northeast winds churn up the sand and drop new teeth on the beach. Winter is prime time.

  3. Find the shell line

    Teeth collect with the shells and gravel where waves drop them. Crouch down and scan the darker piles slowly.

  4. Look for shape and shine

    Fossil teeth are usually black, gray or brown. Look for a glossy little triangle, not the color of a shell.

  5. Bring a sifter

    A simple mesh screen in the shallow surf lets the sand wash through and leaves the teeth behind.

  6. Respect the beach

    Oak Island is a sea turtle nesting beach from May to October. Stay clear of marked nests and leave the dunes alone.

Why are fossil teeth black? Over thousands to millions of years, minerals in the surrounding sediment slowly replace the original tooth material. The color of the teeth comes from those minerals, which is why beach teeth here are usually black, gray or brown, while desert teeth from Peru can be honey-tan.
